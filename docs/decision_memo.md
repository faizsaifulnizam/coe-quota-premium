# Decision memo — Singapore COE: tighter quota, or more bids?

**The answer: more bids, mostly — one clean supply case, and one recent puzzle.** Across 396 auctions since 2010, exercise-to-exercise premium moves are moderately associated with **bids-per-quota** moves (Spearman ρ **+0.37** Cat A / **+0.56** Cat B since May 2022) and only weakly with **quota** moves (ρ **−0.14** / **−0.09**). At the 25 largest premium jumps, quotas were as likely to rise as fall (median change **0.0%**; 12 down, 12 up) while bids-per-quota rose in **19 of 25** — including the two biggest jumps of the series, both in **2024-01 R2**: Cat B **+26,990** (quota +3.8%, bids per quota 1.34 → 1.99) and Cat A **+16,579** (quota −0.1%, 1.28 → 2.04). The one clear quota-cut jump: Cat C **2023-02 (+8,010)** after a **−43.6%** quota cut.

**But the 2024–26 record levels are not a bid flood.** Sep 2026: Cat A record **133,009** (latest exercise 131,890), Cat B **135,001** — arriving while quotas recovered from their lowest-since-2014 levels (Cat A average 549 in 2022 → 1,252 in 2026, +28% vs 2024) and count pressure eased (bids per quota 1.58 → 1.53; success rate 64.0% → 66.1%). The 2022–23 doubling, by contrast, coincided with the squeeze (Cat B average quota 483 in 2023 vs 820 in 2021). Counts alone do not explain the last two years: the premium is set by the marginal successful **bid value**, which this file does not contain.

**Scope & definitions.** LTA COE bidding results via data.gov.sg — one row per category per exercise, 2010-01 → 2026-09; **premium = the quota premium (the lowest successful bid price for the exercise), not the PQP** (the renewal price existing owners pay). Pressure is count-based: `bids per quota = bids received ÷ quota`, `success rate = successful bids ÷ bids received`.

**The May-2022 redefinition, handled.** Category A/B rules changed at the May 2022 1st exercise (observable: Cat A quota +15.0% at the boundary, 532 → 612). The two regimes are never averaged together; the single boundary-crossing change is excluded from statistics and counted (1,975 deltas − 7 excluded = 1,968); every figure carries the break line. Categories C/D/E were not redefined; their split by the same date is a period comparison only.

**What a reader should NOT conclude.** This file cannot say *why* bidders show up, who they are, or what they bid — only how many bids met how many certificates and the price that cleared. It cannot time the market, forecast a premium, or attribute causes; it is descriptive. One source anomaly (Cat D, 2010-01 R2: S$20,090 reverting to S$852 next exercise) stays visible in the data and is noted, not trimmed.
